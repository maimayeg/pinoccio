Researchers warn methane emissions ‘rising faster than ever’

**Methane Emissions Rising Faster Than Ever, Researchers Warn**

Recent studies indicate that global methane emissions are on a trajectory that could surpass previous records, according to a team of researchers at the International Institute for Atmospheric Studies. Methane, a potent greenhouse gas, is recognized for its contributions to climate change and global warming, having a global warming potential many times greater than that of carbon dioxide over a short time frame.

The latest data suggests that methane emissions are rising at a rate of about 15 million metric tons annually, a significant increase from the five million metric ton rise seen in previous years. Scientists point to several contributing factors, including the increase in natural gas production and agricultural practices that emit methane, such as enteric fermentation in livestock.

While the trend is concerning, experts urge a balanced perspective. "Understanding the sources and trends is essential for formulating effective responses," stated Dr. Emily Robinson, a climate scientist involved in the study. Dr. Robinson emphasized the importance of continued monitoring and research to inform mitigation strategies.

According to the Global Methane Tracker, as of 2022, methane accounted for approximately 16% of worldwide greenhouse gas emissions, with 60% of these emissions stemming from human activities. Projections suggest that, without significant intervention, methane levels could continue to rise, potentially reaching unprecedented levels by 2030.

Researchers have pointed out that while methane is indeed a powerful greenhouse gas, reducing its emissions may offer one of the quickest solutions to help slow down climate change. Measures such as improving agricultural practices, minimizing leaks in oil and gas infrastructure, and enhancing waste management systems are being considered by policymakers.

Although these findings suggest a rising concern for the climate, experts stress that the situation is manageable with proactive strategies. They encourage governments, industries, and individuals to collaborate on solutions that effectively target methane emissions while also considering broader environmental objectives.
