**Adekunle Gold on sickle cell advocacy: ‘It’s time to learn my voice – people are dying’**

Nigerian singer-songwriter Adekunle Gold recently spoke out about the urgent need for awareness regarding sickle cell disease (SCD), a genetic disorder that predominantly affects individuals of African descent. His comments came during an interview where he emphasized the importance of understanding and addressing this significant public health issue.

Sickle cell disease affects millions worldwide, with approximately 300,000 babies born with the condition each year according to the World Health Organization (WHO). In Nigeria, where the prevalence of the sickle cell trait is estimated to be around 24%, the disease presents not only a health challenge but also a socioeconomic burden.

Gold pointed out that many people remain unaware of the implications of sickle cell disease, which can lead to severe health complications, including chronic pain, organ damage, and increased risk of infections. The singer noted that, although advances in medical care have improved the lives of patients, many still face significant challenges due to inadequate access to health services, especially in rural areas.

In his advocacy, Gold emphasized the importance of education and awareness campaigns. He explained that by learning about sickle cell disease, individuals can make informed decisions about marriage and procreation. Genetic counseling can play a vital role in this, allowing potential parents to understand their chances of having a child with the condition.

Statistics illustrate the need for increased advocacy: Each year, approximately 100,000 Americans are affected by sickle cell disease, and in low-resource settings, the mortality rate in children under five can be as high as 50% without proper medical care. This emphasizes the critical nature of access to ongoing medical support.

Gold's statement, “It’s time to learn my voice – people are dying,” serves as a call to action. As a public figure, his influence could help shift perceptions and increase attention to this often-overlooked health issue. The necessity for people to understand the implications of sickle cell trait and disease cannot be understated, and initiatives that promote awareness can ultimately save lives.

In conclusion, while there are ongoing challenges related to sickle cell disease, the potential for change through education and advocacy remains promising. Adekunle Gold’s engagement with this cause highlights the power of voices in public health and the importance of community support in facing such issues.
