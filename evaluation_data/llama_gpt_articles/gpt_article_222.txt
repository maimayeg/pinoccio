**This 70-Year-Old Retiree Just Graduated Med School. He Has This Advice for Others**

In a remarkable testament to lifelong learning, 70-year-old Harold Jenkins has recently graduated from the University of Medicine and Health Sciences, making him one of the oldest students to earn a medical degree in the institution’s history. Jenkins, a former accountant, decided to pursue his dream of becoming a physician after retiring and is now preparing to enter the challenging world of medicine.

Jenkins's journey to medical school was a path not without its challenges. He began preparing for the Medical College Admission Test (MCAT) at the age of 66, dedicating hours each day to studying. "I knew it would take a lot of work, but I was determined to show that age is just a number when it comes to education and passion," Jenkins reflected during an interview. His dedication paid off when he was accepted into medical school at 67.

Research shows that the number of older adults pursuing education is increasing, with a 2021 report from the National Center for Education Statistics indicating that the enrollment of students aged 65 and older in degree programs has grown by roughly 30% since 2010. Jenkins's experience fits within this trend, underscoring a shift in societal views regarding older learners.

As he prepares to enter residency, Jenkins highlights several pieces of advice for older individuals considering further education or a career shift. First, he stresses the importance of setting clear goals. "Know what you want to achieve and don't be afraid to go after it," he advises. 

Secondly, Jenkins emphasizes the value of resilience. The rigor of medical training can be intense, challenging not only one’s intellect but also stamina and adaptability. "There will be hard days—just like any journey—but persistence is key," he notes. 

Finally, he encourages building a supportive network. "Surround yourself with people who inspire and uplift you, regardless of their age. I had so many supportive classmates who helped me navigate the tougher times," Jenkins explains.

The implications of his story resonate far beyond individual aspirations. With an aging population and a growing demand for healthcare professionals, Jenkins's pursuit of medicine at an older age could inspire other retirees to consider similar paths. The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects that the healthcare sector will add approximately 2.4 million jobs by 2029, underscoring the potential benefits of increasing diversity in the workforce, including age diversity.
