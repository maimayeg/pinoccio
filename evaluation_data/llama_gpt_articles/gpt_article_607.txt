**Election Officials Raise 'Serious Questions' About US Postal Service Ballot Delivery Plans**

In light of approaching electoral deadlines, several officials from state and local election offices across the country have expressed concerns regarding the United States Postal Service (USPS) plans for ballot delivery in the upcoming elections. With early voting and mail-in ballots proving increasingly popular, the efficiency and reliability of postal services are under scrutiny.

According to recent statistics from the U.S. Election Assistance Commission, approximately 26 million Americans voted by mail alone in the last presidential election—a figure expected to rise this cycle. However, election officials are raising issues about the postal service’s recent operational changes and their potential implications for timely ballot delivery.

The concerns were first highlighted in a report issued by the National Association of Secretaries of State (NASS), which outlined potential risks linked to USPS operational adjustments made earlier in the year. Specifically, officials pointed to alterations in delivery schedules and workforce reductions that might affect the timely processing of ballots. These changes could lead to delays that might prevent some voters from having their say counted in a timely manner.

While recent USPS reports indicate an improvement in overall delivery performance to approximately 93% on-time delivery rates, election officials assert that any delays could prove consequential, especially when ballots must arrive by established deadlines. In several states, ballots must be postmarked by Election Day and received by the close of business within a legally specified time frame, often ranging from a few days to a week after the election. 

Election officials in key states have initiated discussions with USPS representatives to gain clarity on how changes may affect ballot delivery. “Understanding USPS’s operational strategy is critical,” stated a representative from the Michigan Secretary of State’s office. “We need assurance that voters will have their ballots delivered promptly, especially considering the high stakes of this election.”

While USPS has emphasized its commitment to ensuring secure and timely delivery during election periods, the continuing uncertainties have prompted a call for vigilance. Some election experts recommend that voters send their ballots as early as possible or utilize ballot drop boxes when available to mitigate any risks posed by postal delivery.

Despite these warnings, it is important to note that many of the concerns are currently speculative. No evidence to date has suggested that these changes will lead to widespread disenfranchisement. In a country where voting by mail has gained legitimacy, the USPS continues to adapt to changing demands with the goal of maintaining its role as an essential service.
