**Air Canada Says Government Should Be Ready to Prevent Pilots from Striking**

Air Canada has issued a statement indicating that the Canadian government should take proactive measures to prevent a potential strike among its pilots. The airline, which is the largest in Canada, underscored the importance of maintaining seamless operations for the benefit of both customers and the economy.

In their statement, Air Canada referenced ongoing negotiations with the airline’s pilots, who are represented by the Air Canada Pilots Association. The union has expressed concerns regarding compensation and working conditions, as has been common in various industries in recent years. Air Canada emphasized that while negotiations are ongoing, the possibility of a strike could lead to significant disruptions, including flight cancellations, delays, and economic repercussions that could affect both travelers and businesses relying on the airline’s services.

According to industry statistics, air travel contributes significantly to Canada’s economy, supporting approximately 630,000 jobs and generating upwards of $49 billion annually. A strike during a peak travel season could have wider implications, not just for Air Canada, but for the tourism sector and associated businesses across the country. 

Experts suggest that a negotiated agreement is preferable to a work stoppage. While both sides have expressed a willingness to engage in dialogue, the timeline for a resolution remains uncertain. Currently, pilots are operating under the terms of their existing agreement, which is set to expire in the coming months, and both parties are encouraged to reach an agreement to avoid potential disruptions.

Transport Minister Omar Alghabra has stated that the government is monitoring the situation closely and stands ready to intervene if necessary. However, he also emphasized the importance of dialogue and negotiation between the airline and its pilots.

Stakeholders in the industry remain hopeful that a resolution can be reached before any major disruptions occur. The general consensus is that while the threat of a strike is concerning, active negotiations are still ongoing and both parties have expressed a commitment to resolve their issues amicably.
