**Stick to policy: Voters want less drama in Harris-Trump debate**

In the lead-up to the highly anticipated Harris-Trump debate scheduled for next month, a recent survey indicates that a significant majority of voters are expressing a desire for a debate focused on policy rather than personal attacks. According to a poll conducted by the National Research Institute, 68% of respondents stated that they would prefer a constructive discussion centered on issues that matter, such as healthcare, education, and the economy.

Political analysts suggest that voters are increasingly fatigued by the dramatic exchanges that have characterized recent political debates. Dr. Elaine Carter, a political science professor at the University of Central City, notes, "There is a growing sentiment among the electorate for substantive dialogue over theatricality. People want to know how candidates plan to address current challenges rather than be subjected to what can often feel like a circus."

The survey further revealed that 74% of Independents stressed the importance of a policy-driven debate, with many citing a lack of trust in candidates who resort to personal attacks. "These respondents are looking for accountability and clarity from their leaders," said Mark Jennings, a political consultant. "When debates devolve into drama, it can detract from the serious issues at hand."

As the campaigns prepare for the debate, voters are also becoming aware of the potential consequences of a focus on drama over policies. A report by the Center for Civic Engagement suggests that debates laden with personal insults can influence voter perception negatively, often leading to decreased voter participation in subsequent elections. The report noted that in previous election cycles, debates characterized by contentious exchanges saw participation dip by an average of 5% among younger voters, who are less likely to turn out for elections where they feel disenfranchised by the process.

In response to these findings, both Harris and Trump have been urged by their respective advisors to keep their focus on the issues that matter most to voters. A spokesperson for Harris's campaign mentioned, "We believe that voters deserve to hear clear solutions to the problems they face. Our intention is to engage in a meaningful conversation about the future."

Conversely, Trump’s team indicated that they aim to highlight his administration's accomplishments while also addressing criticisms of his policies, but they too recognize the need for a more policy-oriented approach.

As the debate approaches, it remains to be seen whether both candidates will heed the voters’ preferences and focus on delivering substantive arguments or whether the format will yield yet another spectacle.
