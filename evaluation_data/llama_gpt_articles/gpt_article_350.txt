**2024 Atlantic Hurricane Season Fast Facts**

Historical data shows that the Atlantic hurricane season typically spans from June 1 to November 30. Over the past decade, seasons have varied in intensity, with some years experiencing unusually high activity and others witnessing relatively fewer storms. The increase in ocean temperatures, combined with atmospheric conditions such as wind patterns, are believed to contribute to these fluctuations.

Preparedness remains a key focus for coastal residents. Authorities recommend reviewing emergency plans and ensuring that supplies are adequate as the season begins. While forecasts provide an opportunity for proactive measures, it is important to remember that storm paths and intensities can change rapidly.

The economic impact of hurricanes can be substantial, affecting infrastructure, local economies, and insurance markets. The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) continues to emphasize the importance of preparedness and community resilience. Government agencies and local organizations are working collaboratively to provide resources and support to those in potentially affected areas.

While the season ahead promises challenges, an informed and prepared populace can mitigate some of the risks associated with hurricanes.
