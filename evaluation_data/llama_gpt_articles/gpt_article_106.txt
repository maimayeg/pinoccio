**Fact Check: Trump-Harris Presidential Debate — Truths and Falsehoods**

In the lead-up to the upcoming presidential election, the highly anticipated debate between former President Donald Trump and current Vice President Kamala Harris has sparked considerable discussion among voters and political analysts alike. With both candidates representing starkly different visions for the country, the need for an objective assessment of their statements during the debate has become increasingly critical.

During the debate, Trump asserted that "record-low unemployment rates" were achieved under his administration, a statement that merits exploration. According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, the unemployment rate decreased to a historic low of 3.5% by February 2020. However, it is important to note that this trend began during Barack Obama’s presidency. Unemployment reached its lowest levels towards the tail end of the Obama administration and continued to improve somewhat under Trump. Thus, while Trump’s claim captures a significant fact, it oversimplifies the context in which these figures were achieved.

Conversely, Vice President Harris emphasized the need for "accessible healthcare for every American." A recent study by the Kaiser Family Foundation indicates that approximately 29 million Americans are uninsured. While the Biden administration has made strides to expand healthcare coverage, it has not yet reached the goal of universal coverage. Harris's statement, while ambitious and ideally reflective of public sentiment, is not entirely achievable in the immediate term and overlooks existing systemic challenges.

Another notable moment in the debate was Trump’s claim regarding inflation rates, asserting they were "the highest in decades." According to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, consumer prices surged dramatically in 2021, marking the highest inflation rate since 1982. This statistic is supported by various economic analyses and reflects broader economic trends exacerbated by global supply chain interruptions and unprecedented levels of government spending during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Harris responded by stressing that wages have grown for many workers, which the Economic Policy Institute corroborates, indicating that the median wage has indeed seen an upward trend. However, this wage growth has not fully kept pace with inflation, leading to a complex economic scenario for many families.

As both candidates prepare for future debates, voters should remain aware of the complexities and nuances behind the claims made during their exchanges. Engaging with these issues critically will aid in shaping an informed electorate as the election approaches.
