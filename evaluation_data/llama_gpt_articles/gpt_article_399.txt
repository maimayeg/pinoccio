Brazil says it has nearly cleared gold miners from Amazon Yanomami reservation

**Brazil Claims Progress in Clearing Gold Miners from Yanomami Reservation**

Brazil’s government has announced significant advancements in its efforts to remove illegal gold miners from the Amazon Yanomami reservation, an area that is home to one of the world's most vulnerable Indigenous groups. The operation, which began earlier this year, has reportedly led to the removal of a substantial number of miners from Yanomami land.

According to the Brazilian Ministry of Indigenous Peoples, over 90% of the illegal mining operations in the region have been dismantled, resulting in the recovery of thousands of hectares of land. This endeavor is part of a larger strategy to protect Indigenous territories and combat environmental degradation linked to illegal mining activities.

Though the government’s figures indicate considerable progress, advocacy groups remain cautious. They highlight that while significant areas have been cleared, the presence of lingering miners poses ongoing risks to the health and safety of Yanomami communities. Reports suggest that exposure to mercury—a common byproduct of gold mining—continues to affect local waterways, raising concerns over water quality for drinking and fishing.

The Yanomami reservation is not only a crucial ecological zone but also a significant cultural heritage site for the Indigenous people who reside there. Experts emphasize that the long-term effects of mining on both the environment and Indigenous livelihoods are yet to be fully understood. The Brazilian government has stated its commitment to restoring the land and providing support to the Yanomami community, but the timeline and efficacy of these measures remain uncertain.

The international community continues to watch the situation closely, given the implications for both environmental preservation and Indigenous rights. Some organizations call for increased governmental transparency regarding mining concessions and a clearer strategy on post-removal land rehabilitation.

In summary, the Brazilian government's initiatives to remove gold miners from the Yanomami reservation have yielded positive results, highlighted by the removal of a large number of illegal miners. Nonetheless, challenges persist in rehabilitating the land and ensuring the long-term safety of Indigenous populations in the region.
