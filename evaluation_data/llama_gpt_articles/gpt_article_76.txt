**Alberto Fujimori, Ex-President of Peru Jailed for Rights Abuses, Dies at 86**

Alberto Fujimori, the former president of Peru, passed away at the age of 86 while serving a 25-year prison sentence for human rights violations and corruption during his time in office. His death marks the end of a controversial chapter in Peru's history, where his administration (1990-2000) was marked by both economic reforms and significant human rights abuses.

Fujimori, who came to power by running on a platform of economic stabilization and anti-terrorism, implemented critical policies that reduced inflation and improved economic growth in the 1990s. Statistics from the Peruvian economy show a notable decrease in inflation rates from over 7,000 percent in 1990 to around 10 percent just a few years into his presidency. These fiscal policies garnered him a considerable base of support among the middle class and business communities.

However, Fujimori's legacy is equally marred by allegations of systemic human rights violations. Institutions such as the Truth and Reconciliation Commission documented thousands of cases of extrajudicial killings, forced disappearances, and torture linked to policies pursued by his government in the fight against insurgent groups like the Shining Path. The commission reported that more than 69,000 people lost their lives during this period, with a high number of victims being civilians.

Fujimori's attempts to consolidate power also raised concerns. In 1992, he led a self-coup, dissolving Congress and restricting the judiciary, an action that drew criticism both domestically and internationally. His tactics were contentious; while some labeled them necessary for national security, others viewed them as authoritarian.

Following his controversial presidency, Fujimori fled to Japan in 2000 amid growing scandals, including bribery and corruption. He returned to Peru in 2005, was arrested, and faced multiple charges that resulted in significant prison sentences. His legal battles continued up until the time of his death, reflecting the ongoing turmoil and debate regarding his legacy.

His passing has sparked discussions in Peru regarding reconciliation and the need to address the historical injustices of the past. While some Peruvians remember him as a strong leader who brought stability and economic growth, others view him as a symbol of repression and human rights abuses.
