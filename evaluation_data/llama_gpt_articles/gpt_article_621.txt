**Exclusive: US supports two permanent UN Security Council seats for Africa**

In a significant diplomatic development, the United States has officially voiced its support for the addition of two permanent seats for African nations on the United Nations Security Council (UNSC). This move is perceived as a response to increasing calls for reform within one of the most important international governance bodies. Supporters suggest that this change could enhance the representation of the continent’s interests in global decision-making, while critics raise questions about the implications and feasibility of such an initiative.

Historically, Africa has been underrepresented in the UNSC. As of October 2023, there have been 15 member nations, with only three African countries—Egypt, Kenya, and South Africa—serving as non-permanent members. Proponents of the proposed reform argue that permanent seats for African nations would provide these states with a critical platform to address issues such as climate change, regional security, and economic development.

Recent statistics indicate that Africa is home to some of the fastest-growing economies, with a projected growth rate of 4.5% for 2024, according to the International Monetary Fund (IMF). This economic dynamism underscores the relevance of including African nations in high-level discussions regarding international peace and security.

The U.S. decision to support African representation comes amid a broader push for reform in the UNSC. Advocacy groups, scholars, and various governments have consistently highlighted the importance of a more inclusive council that reflects the geopolitical realities of the 21st century. With Africa representing a significant portion of the UN’s member states and holding a diverse range of perspectives on global issues, this support could signify a shift in how global power dynamics are recognized and addressed.

While the U.S. stance has been welcomed by many African leaders, there remains skepticism regarding the potential for effective reform. Questions linger about which countries would occupy these permanent seats, as there is ongoing debate among African states about eligibility and representation.

Additionally, the process of reforming the UNSC is often lengthy and complex due to the need for broad consensus among member states. The U.S. support marks a pivotal step, but experts caution that substantial challenges remain in achieving meaningful change.
