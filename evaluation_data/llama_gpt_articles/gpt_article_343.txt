**Do Not Be Conspicuous: Spanish City Billboards Urge British Tourists to Keep Their Clothes On and Behave**

In an effort to maintain the local culture and enhance the experience of tourism, the coastal city of Marbella in Spain has introduced a series of billboards aimed specifically at British tourists. The billboards, which read "Do Not Be Conspicuous," are part of a broader campaign to encourage visitors to adhere to local customs and exhibit appropriate behavior during their stay.

Local officials noted that tourism contributes significantly to Marbella's economy, accounting for approximately 22% of the city's GDP. However, there has been a noted increase in complaints about public indecency and disruptive behavior linked to a minority of tourists, particularly during peak holiday seasons. In recent years, local law enforcement reported a 15% rise in public disturbance incidents, prompting the city council to take action to safeguard the reputation of the area as a family-friendly destination.

The city’s mayor, Ana Martinez, spoke about the initiative at a recent press conference. "While we welcome tourists, we also value our traditions and residents’ comfort. The campaign is a light-hearted reminder for visitors to enjoy the beautiful climate and beaches while respecting our community standards," she stated. 

Tourism statistics for Marbella show that British visitors comprise nearly 30% of international arrivals, making them a vital demographic for the local economy. However, some local businesses have reported encountering challenges with unruly behavior, particularly in nightlife hotspots and public beaches. As a response, the campaign targets raising awareness rather than restricting freedom, maintaining that the aim is to foster respect for local norms.

In addition to the billboards, the campaign includes public service announcements and educational efforts in hotels, aiming to inform tourists of local customs and expectations. A recent survey indicated that 72% of visitors from the UK expressed a desire to learn more about the cultural practices of the places they visit. This statistic suggests that there is an avenue for improvement in enhancing cross-cultural understanding.

Critics of the campaign have voiced concerns over its necessity, arguing that the majority of British tourists already behave suitably. They contend that focusing on the actions of a small minority could inadvertently perpetuate negative stereotypes about a wider group. The mayor reassured that the campaign is intended not to vilify but to encourage a more respectful interaction between tourists and locals, ultimately enhancing the overall experience for everyone involved.
