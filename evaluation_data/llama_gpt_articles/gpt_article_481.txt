**New US Men’s Soccer Coach Mauricio Pochettino Outlines His Vision Ahead of the Next World Cup: ‘We Need to Think Big’**

In a recent press conference, newly appointed US Men’s National Soccer Team coach Mauricio Pochettino shared his aspirations for the team leading up to the 2026 World Cup, which will be co-hosted by the United States, Canada, and Mexico. Pochettino, known for his strategic acumen and a history of developing young talent, emphasized the importance of a bold vision for the team’s future.

“Success is not just about playing well, it’s about thinking big,” said Pochettino, citing the need for a comprehensive approach that encompasses player development, tactical innovation, and international competitiveness. “We have the opportunity to build a legacy for U.S. soccer, and we must seize that opportunity.”

Statistics indicate that soccer is rapidly growing in popularity in the United States, with recent surveys showing that approximately 23 million Americans consider themselves avid soccer fans. This poses a unique chance for the U.S. Soccer Federation to capitalize on this interest by investing in the national team and grassroots programs.

Pochettino has laid out several key focus areas for the team in preparation for the World Cup. First, he emphasized the importance of nurturing young players through academia and club systems. “We need to create a pipeline of talent from a young age,” he noted, referencing his time at clubs like Southampton and Tottenham Hotspur, where he successfully integrated young players into the first team.

Additionally, the new coach stressed the significance of creating a cohesive team culture. By fostering an environment of collaboration and mutual respect, he believes the team can be more than the sum of its parts. “Teamwork is essential. For us to compete at higher levels, we have to operate as a single unit, not just a collection of individuals,” he mentioned.

Pochettino is also looking to establish a clear tactical identity for the team. He hinted at playing an attractive, possession-based style that could attract more fans to the sport. “To inspire the next generation, we need to be exciting on the field,” he explained. 

While the U.S. Men’s National Team has seen improvements in its performance in recent years, including strong showings in the Gold Cup and World Cup qualifying rounds, the challenge lies in translating this momentum into consistent international success.
