**Kenya Airport Workers Strike Over Takeover Bid by India’s Adani Group**

Kenya’s airport workers have initiated a strike action in response to a proposed takeover bid by the Adani Group, a conglomerate based in India, to acquire a significant stake in Nairobi's Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (JKIA). The industrial action commenced earlier this week and has already disrupted various airport operations, with passengers experiencing delays and cancellations of flights.

The Adani Group expressed its intent to invest in the airport as part of a broader strategy to enhance infrastructure and elevate the airport's status as a regional hub. According to the group, the investment is projected to create thousands of jobs and improve overall service delivery at JKIA. However, airport workers have raised concerns primarily centered on job security and potential changes in working conditions under foreign management.

In a statement released by the Kenya Aviation Workers Union (KAWU), union representatives emphasized that their primary objective is to ensure that the rights and interests of local employees are protected amid the proposed changes in management. They indicated that the number of jobs at stake could exceed 1,500, making the matter critical for many working families in the surrounding areas.

The implications of the strike could extend beyond the immediate inconveniences at the airport. According to the Kenya National Bureau of Statistics, tourism accounted for approximately 8.6% of the country’s GDP in 2021. Continuous disruption at major entry points could potentially have a ripple effect on the economy, further stressing an industry still recovering from the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic. 

While government officials acknowledge the valid concerns raised by workers, they have called for dialogue to explore possible resolutions. Transport Cabinet Secretary Kipchumba Murkomen has urged both parties to engage in discussions, aiming to ensure minimal disruptions while safeguarding the investment that could enhance airport facilities and services in the long run.

Flights have continued to operate on a reduced schedule, and measures are being implemented to manage traffic effectively as the strike unfolds. Stakeholders from the aviation industry are monitoring the developments closely, recognizing the delicate balance between foreign investment opportunities and the rights of local workers.
