**‘I will die trying’: Gambian migrant deportees dream of return to Europe**

In recent years, the perilous journey undertaken by many Gambian migrants seeking a better life in Europe has made headlines. As reports of deportations rise, it has become increasingly common for those affected to express a steadfast determination to return, despite the risks involved.

According to the International Organization for Migration (IOM), an estimated 150,000 migrants from West Africa attempted to reach Europe by boat in 2022, with a significant number hailing from The Gambia. Many cite the search for better job opportunities and a desire for improved living standards as their primary motives for migrating. However, the harsh realities of migration often lead to deportation or abandonment of these aspirations.

For some deported individuals, such as 28-year-old Sidi Touray, the resolve to make another attempt persists. "I will die trying," he states with a calmness that contrasts with the gravity of his words. “I know the risks, but for my family, I must try again.” Touray reflects a sentiment held by many who face the harsh socio-economic conditions back home, where unemployment rates hover around 10%—substantially higher among youth.

Recent statistics illustrate the ongoing challenges these individuals face. The Gambia's youth unemployment rate is significantly higher than the national average, prompting many to consider migration as their only viable option. Cameo reports show that with each closure of borders and implementation of stricter immigration policies in Europe, the stakes for those considering the journey are heightened. 

While the EU has explored various strategies to manage migration, recent initiatives have also aimed at addressing the root causes of migration in countries like The Gambia. Initiatives include investment in local economies, vocational training programs, and partnerships with local governments to create more robust employment opportunities. Yet, for many, these measures feel far removed from the immediate need to support families and make ends meet.

The emotional toll of leaving behind loved ones, paired with the untenable economic circumstances at home, fosters a sense of desperation among many deported individuals. The phenomenon of “circular migration,” where individuals leave, are deported, and then seek to return again, is on the rise. Data from the IOM indicates that nearly 60% of those deported from Europe express a desire to return, highlighting the tenacity of the human spirit.
