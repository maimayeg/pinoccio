**Baby Pygmy Hippo Moo Deng is Thailand’s Latest Online Sensation. But Her Keeper is Worried About Her Fame**

In recent weeks, Moo Deng, a baby pygmy hippopotamus at the Chatuchak Zoo in Bangkok, has captured the hearts of many in Thailand and beyond, emerging as a charming online sensation. Videos of her playful antics, including splashing in her enclosure and enjoying feedings, have gone viral, bringing significant attention to the plight of pygmy hippos and the importance of wildlife conservation. However, her keeper, Nattawut Poonthong, has expressed concerns regarding the responsibilities and potential drawbacks that accompany Moo Deng's newfound fame.

Moo Deng was born just six months ago and weighs a mere 100 pounds. As the population of pygmy hippos is estimated to be under 2,500 in the wild, this species is classified as vulnerable by the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN). The rise of Moo Deng as a social media figure has raised awareness about her species, which can be both advantageous and detrimental. 

While increased visibility can lead to enhanced funding for conservation efforts and animal welfare programs, Nattawut worries that the overwhelming attention may disrupt the natural behaviors and routine of the young hippo. "We have noticed an increase in visitors since her videos went viral, and while this is great for raising awareness, it can also be stressful for Moo Deng," Nattawut explained. He emphasized that pygmy hippos, being nocturnal by nature, require specific conditions to thrive.

Additionally, experts in animal behavior underscore the importance of minimizing human disturbance in animal habitats. Dr. Suda Prakarn, a wildlife biologist, noted that excessive human interaction can lead to stress-related issues, which could affect Moo Deng's development. "It is crucial that the zoo maintains a balance between engaging the public and ensuring that Moo Deng's welfare is prioritized,” she stated.

Furthermore, zoo officials are considering strategies to manage Moo Deng’s exposure to the public effectively. Plans are being discussed to schedule designated feeding sessions, which would allow visitors a chance to view her while ensuring she has plenty of quiet time away from crowds. They are also implementing educational programs to inform the public about pygmy hippos and the conservation challenges they face, aiming to convert the excitement around Moo Deng into support for broader efforts.
