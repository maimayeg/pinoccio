**‘Go around. Go around. Go around’: Pilots who came within 150 feet of a crash share how they deftly averted disaster**

In the world of aviation, safety is a paramount concern, and the phrase “Go around” has been both a command and a mantra for pilots navigating complex landing scenarios. Recently, several pilots have recounted close calls in which they managed to avert potential disasters by executing timely go-arounds during critical landing phases.

A gathering of aviation professionals at the annual Safety in Aviation Symposium highlighted the importance of this command, particularly in high-traffic airports where successful landings are as much a product of strategy as they are of skill. One pilot, who preferred to remain anonymous, recalled an incident where their aircraft was within 150 feet of another plane that had unexpectedly crossed onto the runway. “The instinct kicks in, and we adhere strictly to our training,” they said. “The call to ‘Go around’ was the right one, and it allowed us to regroup and assess our surroundings.”

Statistics indicate that go-arounds are common in multi-runway environments, with approximately 5% of all approaches resulting in a failed landing attempt and a decision to climb back up to await another opportunity. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) reported that the vast majority of these go-arounds are executed safely, with pilots’ training playing a critical role in these high-pressure situations.

Another pilot shared their experience of a near-miss while approaching a busy international airport. “We were about to land when we spotted a vehicle on the runway. It was a moment that could have turned catastrophic, but we had rehearsed this scenario,” they explained. “The go-around decision was made quickly, and it was effective. It’s a reminder that vigilance is key in aviation.”

Experts note that while the aviation system is designed with multiple levels of safety, human factors still play a significant role in incident prevention. “Pilots are trained extensively to recognize and react to developing situations,” said Dr. Ellen Butters, a safety consultant. “What we often hear are stories of decision-making and teamwork in the cockpit that ensure the safety of everyone on board."

Despite the risks associated with flying, the aviation industry continues to improve safety protocols and pilot training to further minimize the chances of near-misses. Regular simulations of emergency scenarios, such as an imminent go-around, are standard practice, bolstering pilots’ ability to respond effectively.
