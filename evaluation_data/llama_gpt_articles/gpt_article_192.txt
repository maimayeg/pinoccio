**People are suffocating: Bedouins in Israel say Gaza war has worsened decades of marginalization**

As the conflict in Gaza escalates, many within Israel's Bedouin community express concerns regarding their situation, which they assert has been exacerbated by the ongoing violence. For decades, the Bedouins, a semi-nomadic group predominantly residing in the Negev region, have experienced marginalization, characterized by limited access to resources and services, economic disadvantages, and social exclusion.

"The environment is stifling," states Fahd Al-Atrash, a community leader from the Negev. "Not only do we feel the weight of the current war, but we have long carried the burden of being overlooked by state institutions." Al-Atrash points to statistics indicating that nearly 70% of Bedouin households live below the poverty line, a figure that has remained stagnant over the years due to systemic neglect.

The Gaza war has drawn attention away from the Bedouins’ challenges, as the international community and local media predominantly focus on the immediate humanitarian needs in Gaza. This shift has led to what some community members term an acute feeling of invisibility. "Our issues did not start with this war; they are part of a larger fabric of neglect and disenfranchisement that predates this conflict," notes Dr. Hanan Salah, a sociologist specializing in Bedouin studies.

Further complicating matters, Bedouins have faced increased restrictions on their movement and access to land, particularly in light of security concerns arising from the ongoing hostilities. According to reports from local NGOs, there has been a significant uptick in land disputes and demolition orders affecting unrecognized villages in recent months. "To feel like your very existence is being questioned, especially at a time when others are rallying for their rights, is suffocating," Al-Atrash remarks.

Despite the difficult circumstances, some Bedouins are taking active steps to address their plight. Efforts to forge alliances with other marginalized groups in Israel have gained momentum, seeking to amplify their voices in a national dialogue. “We believe that by standing together, we can ensure that we aren't lost in the noise of larger conflicts,” shares Amani Abu-Rmeileh, a young activist.
