**Why Does Africa Struggle to Produce Vaccines for Mpox, COVID, and More?**

Despite the urgent need for vaccines in light of recent health crises such as the COVID-19 pandemic and outbreaks of mpox, Africa has faced significant challenges in producing vaccines locally. Examining the multifaceted reasons behind this issue reveals complexities related to infrastructure, funding, and global inequities, all of which contribute to the continent's ongoing struggle.

Historically, Africa has been underrepresented in the global vaccine manufacturing landscape. According to the World Health Organization, only about 1% of vaccines administered in Africa are produced on the continent. This dependency on imports for vaccines leaves African nations vulnerable to external market fluctuations and political dynamics that can disrupt supply chains.

One primary obstacle is the lack of advanced manufacturing facilities. A 2021 report by the African Union indicated that less than 10 countries on the continent possess the capacity to produce vaccines, primarily focusing on existing public health needs. This limited capacity hinders scalability, particularly during health emergencies when rapid production is essential. Furthermore, patients and healthcare systems require not just vaccines but also comprehensive healthcare support to manage the distribution and administration processes effectively.

Funding also plays a crucial role in vaccine production. According to the African Development Bank, investment in biotechnology and pharmaceutical sectors remains limited, with only 5% of all health-related funding allocated to innovative health solutions. This financial inadequacy stifles the growth of local vaccine manufacturers, preventing them from expanding operations or investing in new technology essential for vaccine development.

Additionally, intellectual property barriers tied to vaccine production further complicate the situation. Although the COVID-19 pandemic prompted discussions about waiving intellectual property rights for vaccines, progress has been slow, maintaining the status quo and limiting local manufacturers' ability to produce vaccines that meet international safety and efficacy standards.

Continued advancements in local vaccine research and development are promising. Initiatives such as the Africa CDC's partnership with various stakeholders aim to bolster vaccine manufacturing capacity on the continent. Additionally, the mRNA technology transfer hub established in South Africa represents a significant step towards enhancing local vaccine production capabilities.

While the challenges ahead are considerable, the potential benefits of increased vaccine production in Africa cannot be overstated. By enhancing local manufacturing, African nations can improve their health security, access to vaccines, and ultimately their ability to respond to future health emergencies effectively.
